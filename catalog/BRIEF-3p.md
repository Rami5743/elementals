## Idea keys no 3-player level has touched

- `belt-filter` — A belt whose speed sits between two elements' run speeds, so some can walk against it and some never can.
- `belt-reverse` — One player decides which way everyone else is carried.
- `hop-beats-belt` — A hop breaks contact with a belt that cannot be walked against.
- `lift-loop` — A loop lift that will not wait: boarding and unloading inside the pause.
- `fan-band` — One updraft that does something different to each element.
- `glide-passenger` — Air carries somebody across a gap nobody else can cross.
- `seesaw-balance` — A plank whose heavier half sinks: weight has to be arranged, and moving changes it.
- `pound-chain` — Several cracked floors stacked or offset, broken in one descent.
- `pound-to-move` — Pounding the floor out from under an object instead of under yourself.
- `boulder-float` — A boulder rests on the surface of a liquid; pushed in, it becomes floor.
- `boulder-carry` — Something that falls on a player rides on their head until a low roof scrapes it off.
- `freeze-invert` — Ice until Water stands on the pad, and ice again the moment Water steps off.
- `turbine-out-of-reach` — A switch only one element answers to, placed where that element cannot get on its own.
- `wide-door` — A door that opens sideways: a wall that becomes a bridge, or the reverse.
- `dark` — The level is dark; lit torches are the only light, and the map has to be learned.
- `liquid-lane` — Each element wades its own liquid to something the others need.
- `lock-per-element` — One barrier per element, each opened by exactly one of them.
- `crate-step` — A crate pushed into place as the step that makes a height reachable.
- `low-roof-no-jump` — A corridor one tile high: nobody can jump in it at all, so the answer must be a floor.
- `trigger-from-above` — A switch worked from one storey to open a route on another.
- `twist-sacrifice` — A resource is spent: once used, that route or object is gone for good.
- `two-is-not-heavy` — A heavy plate takes max(mass), not the sum: two light players standing on it together do nothing, so the weight has to be fetched.
- `switch-in-mid-flight` — A plate hanging in the air over a gap, armed only by passing through it mid-jump.
- `door-pushes-you-through` — A closing door pushes a player down through a one-way tile — the only way down there, and you cannot do it to yourself.
- `trip-plate-in-the-shaft` — A plate hanging in a lift shaft, pressed by the rider for as long as the lift takes to carry them past it.
- `partner-builds-your-landing` — The place you are going to does not exist yet: a partner has to put it there while you are already in the air.
- `sink-as-a-timer` — A draught too weak to lift but strong enough to slow a fall: the long sink past a column of switches is the window a partner crosses in. (Campaign: Slow Fall.)
- `feed-the-engine-mid-ride` — A machine that runs only for a moment after each touch, so the rider has to keep leaving it and coming back to keep it going. (Campaign: Keep It Spinning.)
- `lift-sorts-by-weight` — A lift that can be told to stop for heavy loads and carry light ones past, so who gets off where is a setting rather than a choice. (Campaign: Weighbridge.)
- `stock-before-it-seals` — A timed airlock whose two gates are never open together: everything that will be needed on the far side — partner, cargo, gems — has to be inside before the clock runs out, and the switch cannot be reached from in there.
- `send-one-through` — A lock that needs every hand but one held on it, so exactly one player can pass — and has to open the way back for the rest from the far side.
- `lift-sheds-its-load` — A lift that rises through a one-way grating is a delivery chute, not a shuttle: it cannot put anything down while it is holding it, and only the trip back down leaves the load standing on the grating. People step off at the top and drop back through at will, so the route is two-way for them and one-way for cargo — and raising the lift again picks the cargo straight back up off whatever it was resting on.
- `single-file-stone` — A boulder in a corridor one tile high is an absolute wall that only Earth can move and only ever forwards, so the one stretch of raised roof is the only place anybody can get past it: where the stone is parked decides who can be on which side of it.
- `stand-aside-is-the-switch` — In a corridor where nobody can pass anybody, the one place to get out of the walking line is a shelf above it — and that shelf is the switch, so standing aside and holding the far door open are the same act, and whoever does it cannot be the one who goes.
- `no-overtaking` — A corridor one tile high is one body wide as well: nobody can jump in it and nobody can hop over anybody, so a party inside it is a queue. The order you file in is the order you are stuck with; the only way to change it is to walk the whole corridor back out; and anyone who comes round from the far end can only ever join at the back. That decides who can reach which switch in the corridor, and so who holds and who goes.
- `unload-the-pan-you-ride` — A seesaw used as the only route between three storeys, one flush with each of its positions. Only the heavier pan is ever at floor level, so the far pan can never be loaded from below: a weight has to be put on your OWN pan to send theirs up to the storey where it can be loaded at all, and that same weight has to be shed again before your pan will carry you anywhere. You rise by what is taken off, never by what is added. (The campaign's scales — Assay, Counterpoise — sum weights too, but to balance a pan under a floating plate or to hoist cargo past a ledge; here the sum decides which storey each of you can reach, and the beam has no switch and no wiring at all.)
- `mass-adds-on-the-beam` — A seesaw compares the SUM of everything on each pan — bodies, cargo, riders standing on other riders — while a heavy plate reads only max(mass), the heaviest single thing on it. The same two bodies are worth 3 to the beam and 2 to the plate; two crates weigh exactly what Earth weighs; and the stone that is merely one side of a sum on the beam is an unforgeable key on the pad. (Campaign: Assay, Counterpoise.)
- `two-in-the-air-at-once` — A lock that counts bodies off the ground: its plates hang in a draught where nobody can stand, one above the other, so each is on only while somebody is in flight inside it, and the door wants both at the same moment. Nobody can be in two of them, so the party has to launch together on a count — and because a draught turns a jump from half a second into a slow sail, their overlap is a window a fourth player can walk through. The weight that starts the draught is the one body the draught will never lift, so the player who needs the door is the player who can never be one of the two in the air.
- `plank-sums-plate-maxes` — The game's two weighing machines disagree about what a crowd weighs. A heavy plate reads max(mass) and ignores riders, so two light players on it are worth one light player; a balance plank reads the sum of each half, recursively through heads, so two of them weigh exactly what Earth weighs and three outweigh Earth. The same bodies are therefore interchangeable with the stone on the scales and worthless on the lock, which decides which of the two jobs the one heavy player has to do in person. And because inequality gives the same full swing whatever the margin, the plank has only three states, of which 'level' is an exact arithmetic coincidence rather than anything a switch can cause.
- `grating-memory-with-erase` — An elevator writes a stone onto a one-way grate; a separate closing press erases it by pushing the stone through after the elevator is removed. Two heavy contacts alternate, storing the previous physical state rather than merely following a switch.
