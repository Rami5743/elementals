## Idea keys no 2-player level has touched

- `lift-as-floor` — The lift platform is also the floor of a corridor, so sending it away takes the road with it.
- `fan-band` — One updraft that does something different to each element.
- `seesaw-balance` — A plank whose heavier half sinks: weight has to be arranged, and moving changes it.
- `pound-chain` — Several cracked floors stacked or offset, broken in one descent.
- `boulder-float` — A boulder rests on the surface of a liquid; pushed in, it becomes floor.
- `boulder-carry` — Something that falls on a player rides on their head until a low roof scrapes it off.
- `one-way-drop` — Down drops you through a ledge: a route that only runs one way.
- `turbine-out-of-reach` — A switch only one element answers to, placed where that element cannot get on its own.
- `torch-bridge` — A torch wired to something that exists only while it burns.
- `lock-per-element` — One barrier per element, each opened by exactly one of them.
- `crate-step` — A crate pushed into place as the step that makes a height reachable.
- `two-is-not-heavy` — A heavy plate takes max(mass), not the sum: two light players standing on it together do nothing, so the weight has to be fetched.
- `door-pushes-you-through` — A closing door pushes a player down through a one-way tile — the only way down there, and you cannot do it to yourself.
- `tower-presses-a-column` — Switches stacked up a wall that nothing but a tower of players reaches, with the order of the tower forced by what stands at the bottom. (Campaign: Totem.)
- `lift-sorts-by-weight` — A lift that can be told to stop for heavy loads and carry light ones past, so who gets off where is a setting rather than a choice. (Campaign: Weighbridge.)
- `send-one-through` — A lock that needs every hand but one held on it, so exactly one player can pass — and has to open the way back for the rest from the far side.
- `hoist-one-way-for-cargo` — A lift that runs up through a one-way grating: whatever it carries is scraped off on the grating when the lift goes back down, so the hoist delivers upward and only upward. People can drop back through after it; cargo never can, so anything sent up is up for good, and every job it still owed the floor below has to have been done first.
- `lift-sheds-its-load` — A lift that rises through a one-way grating is a delivery chute, not a shuttle: it cannot put anything down while it is holding it, and only the trip back down leaves the load standing on the grating. People step off at the top and drop back through at will, so the route is two-way for them and one-way for cargo — and raising the lift again picks the cargo straight back up off whatever it was resting on.
- `no-overtaking` — A corridor one tile high is one body wide as well: nobody can jump in it and nobody can hop over anybody, so a party inside it is a queue. The order you file in is the order you are stuck with; the only way to change it is to walk the whole corridor back out; and anyone who comes round from the far end can only ever join at the back. That decides who can reach which switch in the corridor, and so who holds and who goes.
- `two-stops-no-middle` — A conveyor with no off switch, only a direction: the one load it carries is always being driven against one buffer stop or the other and is never anywhere in between. Each stop is a switch, so the two machines wired to them can never both be powered — the lever does not turn anything on, it decides which end of the level has power. Everything one end owes has to be finished before the stone can be spent on the other, and the end the level has to finish in is not the end it has to be worked in.
- `two-in-the-air-at-once` — A lock that counts bodies off the ground: its plates hang in a draught where nobody can stand, one above the other, so each is on only while somebody is in flight inside it, and the door wants both at the same moment. Nobody can be in two of them, so the party has to launch together on a count — and because a draught turns a jump from half a second into a slow sail, their overlap is a window a fourth player can walk through. The weight that starts the draught is the one body the draught will never lift, so the player who needs the door is the player who can never be one of the two in the air.
- `dive-ahead-of-the-runaway` — A rolling boulder holds 4 px a tick and nobody runs faster than 3.4, so once it is loose it can never be caught from behind: whoever is going to stop it has to already be standing in its path. And the one switch that looses it is the same switch that opens the only way down to that path, so nobody can be waiting there in advance — the way to get in front of the stone is to let it go first and then throw yourself down after it, arriving by falling because falling beats any road.
- `threshold-is-a-switch` — A plate under an exit, so standing in your own front door is a switch. Arriving home is not the end of a player's job but the start of it: the one machine in the level is powered by a body in a doorway, that body can never step out again, and the party has to work out who is going to spend the level being the power supply — and has to get that player to that doorway by a route only they can take before anybody else can move at all. Turned round at the end: the last thing to do is to give up the switch holding a partner's gate open, because a door that shuts on somebody already standing in their own exit costs nothing.
- `plank-sums-plate-maxes` — The game's two weighing machines disagree about what a crowd weighs. A heavy plate reads max(mass) and ignores riders, so two light players on it are worth one light player; a balance plank reads the sum of each half, recursively through heads, so two of them weigh exactly what Earth weighs and three outweigh Earth. The same bodies are therefore interchangeable with the stone on the scales and worthless on the lock, which decides which of the two jobs the one heavy player has to do in person. And because inequality gives the same full swing whatever the margin, the plank has only three states, of which 'level' is an exact arithmetic coincidence rather than anything a switch can cause.
