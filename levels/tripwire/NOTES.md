# Tripwire (`tripwire`) — all four

**Introduces:** a switch hanging in a lift shaft. Nothing can stand on it, and the lift itself does
not press it — only a rider does, and only for as long as the lift takes to carry them past it.

## The intended solution

1. **Air** takes its gem and stands on the turbine. The gate to the shaft latches open.
2. **Water** puts out the flames across the hall, and **Fire** melts the ice behind them. Fire then
   waits five tiles short of the gate at the far end of the hall.
3. **Earth** walks into the shaft while the lift is resting at its berth, and rides. The plate in
   the shaft is heavy, so Earth is the only player who can press it — and Earth presses it from the
   moment its body reaches the plate to the moment the lift has carried it clear.
4. For that stretch of the ride, the gate at the end of the hall stands open. **Fire** has to be
   moving before it is fully up, and through it before the lift goes by.
5. Earth rides on to the top of the shaft and steps off into the loft, where its own door is. The
   lift keeps shuttling, so if Fire missed the gate it simply waits for the next lap.

## Why each player is needed

- **Air** is the only thing a turbine answers to, and the shaft is shut without it.
- **Water** puts out the flames; **Fire** melts the ice. Both are three tiles tall under rock, in
  series, and Water's own door is behind the ice, so Fire's job is not optional.
- **Earth** is the only player heavy enough to press the plate in the shaft, and the gate has no
  other input.

## The insight

The lift looks like transport and the plate looks out of reach. Neither is quite true: the ride
*is* the switch, and the only thing it carries that matters is weight. Once that is clear, the shape
of the level follows — the heavy one has to be the passenger, which means the heavy one cannot be
the runner, which means somebody has to be standing ready at the other end of the hall before the
ride even starts.

## Likely failures and how players recover

- **Missing the window.** The lift is on a loop and never stops; the gate opens again on the next
  lap. Nothing is lost.
- **Setting off too late.** The gate takes a moment to rise and shuts as soon as the lift has
  carried Earth clear, so the run has to start while it is still opening. Again: next lap.
- **Earth arriving at the berth when the lift has gone.** It waits; the lift comes back and pauses
  a second and a half at the bottom.
- **Fire going through and then dawdling.** The gate shutting behind it does not matter — Fire's
  door is on that side.

## Shortcuts that were closed

- The plate in the shaft is heavy, so neither Fire, Water nor Air presses it, and a rider standing
  on another's head does not help: a plate takes the largest single mass overlapping it.
- The lift is not an actor, so it does not press the plate by itself — verified in the simulation.
- The gate, the ice and the flames are all three tiles tall under rock.
- The loft is sealed from the hall by six rows of rock; the lift is the only way to it, and the only
  way out of it is back down the shaft.

## Kinship with the campaign

Catch the Lift and Paternoster are about a lift that will not wait for you. Here the lift waits all
day — what will not wait is the switch it drags past, and the player who has to use that switch is
nowhere near it.
