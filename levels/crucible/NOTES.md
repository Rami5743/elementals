# Crucible (`crucible`) — all four

**Introduces:** a gale strong enough to carry a passenger, used twice — Fire takes Earth up, Air
takes Water.

1. **Solution.** Fire melts the ice that shuts the flue and steps clear of it; Earth climbs onto
   Fire's head. Air, waiting for them, hops into the turbine at the far end — the only thing in
   the level it alone can reach — and the gale latches on. Fire carries Earth sixteen tiles up the
   flue, taking the neutral gem, and lands in the loft. Air takes its own gem, comes back to the
   flue and carries Water up the same way. In the loft Water puts out the flames, Earth takes its
   gem and ground-pounds the cracked floor, and all four drop into the vault below.

2. **Roles.** Fire opens the flue and is the first lift. Air starts the gale and is the second
   lift. Water opens the loft. Earth opens the vault.

3. **Why each is necessary.** Neither Earth nor Water rises in any draught by itself, and a gale
   at this strength carries exactly one passenger — so two carriers are needed, and only Fire and
   Air can be them. The ice, the flames and the vault floor are each one element's business, and
   the turbine answers only to Air. `check` confirms all four counterfactuals.

4. **The insight.** It is not a lift for four, it is a lift for two pairs, and the pairing is
   forced: the two who can rise are the two who must carry.

5. **Likely failures and recovery.** The gale latches on, so it never has to be started twice, and
   a passenger who slips off lands back in the hall and climbs on again. The level has no liquid
   and nothing that can be spent; the only one-way move is the pound at the end.

- **Shortcuts considered and blocked.** The loft is sixteen tiles above the hall with solid rock
  either side of the flue — past a jump, a boost and a four-player tower. The ice and the flames
  reach the roof, and the loft floor is solid everywhere but the three cracked tiles.
