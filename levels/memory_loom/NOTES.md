# Memory Loom

**Introduces:** Three players operate a mechanical memory in which the same ratio retains whichever height was previously written.

## The intended solution

1. Earth turns A on. Fire crosses the upper gate and boards beside the stone, so the loaded platform descends.
2. Fire sets the lower lever and returns to the low platform. The stone retains its low state after Fire unloads. Water reaches B; Earth reaches its first relay.
3. Water turns B on. Together A and B disengage the mechanical advantage. Fire and stone rise. The lower lever and high contact latch Water's second gate.
4. Fire sets its upper-right lever. Water turns B off and Earth turns A off. Fire boards and writes the low state again.
5. Low contact, Fire's upper lever and Earth's relay latch the next Water gate. Water sets C and returns to B. Fire returns to the platform.
6. Water and Earth restore B and A. Fire rises and crosses the upper gate homeward. Earth turns A off after Fire unloads; the stone retains its high position.
7. Water returns to the far end, where the high contact opens the acknowledgment gate, sets the acknowledgment, and returns to B to turn it off.
8. Earth crosses its inverted gate and reaches its final lever, opening the other exits. Fire uses the recall lift as a bridge; everyone goes home.

## Why each player is needed

Fire writes memory and operates height-dependent levers. Water supplies B, C and the high acknowledgment. Earth supplies A, a low-phase relay and final permission. Rock separates galleries; the heavy contacts cannot be replaced by Fire or Water.

## The insight

The lever positions alone do not determine the output. The height remembers a previous load. The stone must fulfill incompatible low and high requests; restore the ratio after unloading.

## Likely failures and how players recover

Leaving the snatch block engaged prevents erasure. Restore A and B. Erasing too early can be recovered via the independent recall lift, with five-second dwell. A low-state attempt at the high acknowledgment is blocked; write high first. Latches retain earlier permissions. These recovery routes are intended, not an exhaustive proof of every physical state.

## Shortcuts that were closed

Direct walking and leaving the rope low fail. All idle-at-exit checks fail. Separate galleries and full-height doors prevent player substitution and head boosts.

## Kinship with the campaign

Weighbridge uses a ratio to select a stop. Here balance retains height and three galleries exchange mutually incompatible permissions; an extension of Rope Register to three operators.
