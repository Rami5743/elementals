# One Through (`one_through`) — all four

**Introduces:** a lock that wants three keys held at the same time, in a level with four players.
It does not latch, so the three who hold it cannot also walk through it — exactly one of you can,
and which one is decided by the keys, not by you.

## The intended solution

1. **Water** puts out the flames at the mouth of the alcove — nobody else can — hops the shaft in
   its floor, takes its gem and stands on the plate at the far end.
2. **Earth** takes its gem and settles on the heavy plate. Nothing lighter will hold it.
3. **Air** takes its gem and settles on the turbine. Nothing else will turn it.
4. With all three held, the lock opens. **Fire** — the one nobody needs as a key — hops over the
   two holding their plates and walks through. It cannot hop through: the lock is two tiles under
   a rock lintel.
5. Beyond, Fire melts the ice and throws the lever. That latches open a door in the crawl under the
   hall, and the crawl runs from the alcove at one end to a ledge in the hall floor at the other.
6. The three let go — the lock shuts behind them, and it does not matter. They drop into the crawl
   through the shaft in the alcove floor, go along it, and jump up through the ledge into the far
   end of the hall. All four walk to their doors.

## Why each player is needed

- **Water** is the only one who can put out the flames, and the third key is behind them.
- **Earth** is the only one heavy enough for the heavy plate.
- **Air** is the only thing a turbine answers to.
- **Fire** is needed precisely because it is not needed as a key: somebody has to go through, and
  the lever beyond the lock is the only way the other three ever get out of the near half.

## The insight

The reflex with a three-key lock is to work out who stands where and then walk through together.
Here the keys are element-locked, so the roster is forced — and the moment you have it, you notice
that it uses three of the four of you and the lock closes the instant anyone steps off. The level is
not "open the lock", it is "decide what the one person who gets through is for".

## Likely failures and how players recover

- **Everyone standing on a plate.** The lock opens and nobody is free to use it. Someone has to get
  off, and the only one who can is Fire.
- **Fire going first and waiting beyond the lock.** Fine — the lock is not latched, so the three can
  take their time.
- **Trying to hop through the lock.** The lintel stops you. Walk.
- **Dropping into the crawl before the lever is thrown.** You get as far as the door under the
  flames and come back; the shaft in the alcove floor is two tiles deep and easy to climb out of.
- Nothing here can be made unwinnable: every switch can be stepped off and stood on again.

## Shortcuts that were closed

- The lock is two tiles tall with a rock lintel over it, so no jump and no boost from a partner's
  head clears it.
- The crawl under the hall is blocked at its second tile by the door Fire opens, so nobody can use
  it to get past the flames or the lock early.
- The heavy plate cannot be held by two light players standing together, or by one standing on the
  other: a plate reads the largest single mass on it.
- The ice beyond the lock is three tiles tall under rock, so the lever cannot be reached without
  Fire.

## Kinship with the campaign

Four Corners and Quorum both want several plates held at once, and both let the group walk through
together once they are. This one is built so that it never can: the lock is one key short of the
party, and the whole level is the consequence.
