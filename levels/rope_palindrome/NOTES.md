# Rope Palindrome

**Introduces:** A four-operator mechanical memory: the same balanced ratio retains the last written height, while three independent operators must cooperate to erase it.

## The intended solution

1. Earth turns A on. Fire crosses the upper gate and adds its weight beside the stone, writing the low state.
2. Fire sets the lower lever, enabling Earth's relay and Air's D lever. Air turns D on. Water reaches B.
3. Fire boards the low platform again. Water turns B on. With A, B and D all on, the snatch block is disengaged and Fire rises with the stone.
4. Fire sets the upper-right lever. Water switches B off and Earth switches A off; Fire boards and writes the low state again. The stone's low contact plus the two relays latch the Water passage.
5. Water sets C and returns to B. Fire boards; Water and Earth restore B and A, bringing Fire and the stone upstairs.
6. Fire crosses the upper gate to the home side. Air turns D off and Earth turns A off after unloading, leaving the high memory intact.
7. Water sets the high acknowledgment and returns to turn B off. Air's inverted gate now opens, followed by its remembered-permission gate.
8. Air sets the final lever, opening the other players' exits. Fire crosses the recall lift at the upper stop; all collect gems and exit.

## Why each player is needed

Fire writes low and operates levers at both heights. Water operates B, C and high acknowledgment. Earth controls A and the low-phase relay. Air contributes the third ratio input D and final permission. Each player's idle-at-exit test fails.

## The insight

The snatch block can balance the stone at either height. The trio A, B, D erases the low state, but the trio must then release its inputs only after the writer has unloaded. Four operators cannot substitute for one another across isolated galleries.

## Likely failures and how players recover

Leaving D off prevents erasure; leaving D or B on blocks Air's final gate. Restore the input combination, unload, then turn the inputs off. The independent recall lift provides a recovery route after premature erasure. Latches preserve earlier permissions. No timed switch, lethal gap or precision jump is required. These recovery routes are intended, not an exhaustive proof of every physical state.

## Shortcuts that were closed

The direct route and retaining the low state both fail. Full-height doors, separate galleries and heavy contacts prevent the obvious substitution shortcuts.

## Kinship with the campaign

Weighbridge uses ratio to select a destination; here equilibrium stores a previous destination. This is the four-player counterpart of Rope Register and Memory Loom, with a third ratio input and a final two-input inversion.
