# Lockstep (`lockstep`) — all four

**Introduces:** a span that has to be summoned and then beaten — a wide door wired to a timed
button and inverted, so pressing the button lays the bridge for four and a half seconds.

1. **Solution.** Water puts out the flames at the near end and takes the two gems on that side.
   Earth presses the button and runs the span first, because it is the slowest; Fire follows on
   its own press, then Water, then Air. Each of them is on their own clock. On the far bank Air
   takes its gem and hops into the turbine, which latches the gate open; Fire melts the ice beyond
   it; Earth ground-pounds the cracked floor and all four drop into the vault.

2. **Roles.** Water opens the near end, Earth opens the vault, Fire opens the ice, Air opens the
   gate — and each of them has to make the crossing on their own legs.

3. **Why each is necessary.** Eight tiles of sludge kill everybody and there is nothing over them
   until the button is pressed. The flames, the ice, the turbine's gate and the vault floor are
   each exactly one element's business. `check` confirms all four counterfactuals.

4. **The insight.** The span is nobody's favour to anybody: there is no holding it open for a
   friend, so the team's only decision is the order, and the order is decided by who runs slowest.

5. **Likely failures and recovery.** Being on the span when it opens drops you in the sludge and
   restarts the level; the button is three tiles from the edge, which is the margin. The button
   can be pressed as often as you like and the gate latches once Air has spun the turbine, so
   nothing is spent.

- **Shortcuts considered and blocked.** The sludge runs the full width between the banks and
  nothing can be pushed into it. The flames, the gate and the ice all reach the roof; the turbine
  is five tiles clear of the gate; and the far bank's floor is solid but for the three cracked
  tiles.
