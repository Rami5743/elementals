# Crosswire (`crosswire`) — Fire + Air

**Introduces:** a crossing whose far end does not exist when you set off. One lever drives two doors
the opposite way round, and it has to be thrown twice — both times while Fire is in mid-air and can
no longer do anything about it.

## The intended solution

The draught is set at 1.54: it lifts Air outright, and it does not lift Fire at all — it holds Fire
at the height it jumped from and carries it about fourteen tiles sideways. That is the whole map.

1. **Air** starts the draught at the turbine, walks off the lip into it and rides up the shaft into
   the loft, fetches its gem at the far end and comes back to the lever.
2. With the lever up, the **shutter** standing in the middle of the gap is open and the **perch** on
   the far side is not there. That is the right state to set off into and the wrong state to arrive
   in.
3. **Fire** runs off the near ledge into the draught and floats. It passes the shutter's column
   about a second in.
4. **Air** throws the lever while Fire is past the shutter and still short of the far side. The
   shutter drops shut behind Fire, and the perch appears under where Fire is about to come down.
   Thrown early, the shutter is in Fire's way; thrown late, there is nothing to land on.
5. Fire lands on the perch, walks the far ledge, takes its gem and throws the lever at the end,
   which opens the cage across the loft.
6. Fire walks back to the near lip of the perch and launches again, this time leftwards. **Air**
   throws the lever a second time once Fire is airborne — the shutter lifts for the return, and the
   perch vanishes, which no longer matters.
7. Fire floats home to the near ledge. Air walks through the opened cage, drops down the chute to
   the far ledge, and both are in their doors.

## Why each player is needed

- **Air** is the only one the draught lifts, so the loft and the lever are its alone, and the lever
  is the only thing that moves either door.
- **Fire** is the only one the draught carries *across*, and the lever at the far end — the one that
  lets Air out of the loft — can only be reached from over there.
- Neither can do the other's half, and neither can be anywhere near the other while it happens.

## The insight

The lever is not a switch you throw to open the way: it is a switch that swaps which half of the way
exists. There is no state of the level in which the whole crossing is there at once, so the crossing
has to be made in two halves with the change happening underneath you — and the person making the
change cannot see what it is like to be the one falling.

## Likely failures and how players recover

- **Throwing the lever too early.** The shutter closes in Fire's face; Fire drops into the sludge.
  The checkpoint is on the far ledge once Fire has been across once; before that it is the start.
- **Throwing it too late.** Fire reaches the far side with nothing under it and falls. Same cost.
- **Not throwing it at all.** Fire floats into the gap and falls; the draught never stops, so the
  next attempt is a walk away.
- **Fire launching the return from the far lip of the perch.** The jump is not long enough to clear
  the perch itself and it lands back on it. Walk to the near lip first.
- **Air flipping before Fire is airborne on the return.** The perch disappears from under Fire's
  feet. Wait until Fire has jumped.

## Shortcuts that were closed

- The gap is twelve tiles of sludge with no floor, and Fire's jump is under three: the draught is
  the only way across, in either direction.
- The loft is reachable only through the narrow shaft at the left of the draught — the rest of the
  draught is capped by the loft's own floor, so Air has to enter at the correct end.
- The cage is a plain door with no latch on the loft side; nothing in the loft opens it.
- The shutter is five tiles tall and spans the whole flight height, so there is no floating over or
  under it.

## Kinship with the campaign

Thermals has a draught that carries rather than lifts, and Crosswind has one element ferrying
another. Neither of them asks a player to commit to a crossing that is not finished yet, or gives
the unfinished half to somebody who is somewhere else.
