## The design, in two sentences

A rolling boulder can only ever be brought to a halt by a body already standing in its path —
nothing in the game catches a runaway from behind — and it comes to rest in the cell immediately
*uphill* of that body, which is exactly where a heavy plate hangs over the walkway, out of reach of
anybody light enough to be standing there. Earth has to fetch the stone up the whole hill and then
loose it, which is precisely why Earth can never be the brake (you push from above and hold from
below), and the brake has to park it high to hold the hatch open long enough for Earth to walk into
the anvil bay it can never leave, then walk the stone down to the low berth for the wicket.

## Measured facts this design rests on (probed, 2026-11-25)

- A boulder with `rolls: true` starts rolling from rest on a `\` slope with no push, and reaches
  4 px/tick (PHYSICS.md agrees).
- A body standing on the slope stops it. It comes to rest in cells `(C-1, R-1)` and `(C, R-1)`
  where the body stands in cell `(C, R)` — i.e. uphill and one row up, **not** on the body's head.
- A **heavy** plate placed in that cell reads the stone and is *not* pressed by the light body
  standing below it.
- Step aside and the stone rolls on at once, and follows the brake downhill: nothing is ever lost.
- On the **flat**, a body stops a rolling stone dead in front of it and it never moves again.
- A plate on the stone's path is pressed *momentarily* as it rolls past (~8 ticks).
- **Earth can push a rolling boulder up a `\` slope**, at about 2.5 px/tick, and it stays put once it
  reaches flat ground. This is what makes every mistake recoverable.
- Earth cannot walk round a boulder on flat ground: it goes up onto its back and off the far side,
  which is what the two scripted hops do.
- A one-tile-high *sloped* corridor is impassable to everything (each column's free cell is a
  separate pocket behind a rock corner), so the hill has three tiles of headroom instead.

## What fails

- Nothing. The one soft target missed is duration: 22.2 s clears the 20 s floor but is short of the
  30 s mark. Every hard requirement is met — four map states, two machines whose interaction is the
  puzzle (an unlatched hatch that only a genuinely stationary stone holds open, and a three-contact
  latched wicket), Earth sealed in the bay for good, the give-up-the-high-berth twist, and one stone
  with two claims on it. Buying the extra seconds would have meant shifting every coordinate on the
  map for about three more, which was not worth the risk to a working level.

## If someone picks this up again

The cheapest honest way to reach 30 s would be to deepen the hill by two tiles using the three spare
rock rows at the bottom (`i = 0..13`, run floor row 20, wicket column 35, exits 36 and 38). The
berth cells do not move, because the hill's top is unchanged — only the run and the exit hall shift.
