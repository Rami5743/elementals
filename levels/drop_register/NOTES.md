# Drop Register

Batch-90: 68/90

**Introduces:** the same boulder is initially an upper stationary permission and, after Earth destroys its support, becomes the movable state of a lower reversible conveyor. This is an irreversible reconfiguration of the electrical circuit, not a succession of similar descents.

## The intended solution
1. The intact slab holds the stone against the upper contact, granting Fire entry to the approval station.
2. Fire records the first approval, allowing Water to enter its station.
3. Water records the second approval. The slab chamber now opens; early release before both approvals is physically blocked.
4. Earth pounds the slab beside the stone. Both descend, but into opposite compartments. The stone lands on the lower conveyor, and the old upper contact is permanently deactivated, opening the complementary exits from the approval stations.
5. The conveyor carries the stone west. Fire and Water enter their new workshops and record both certificates.
6. Fire returns to the direction lever and reverses the belt, sending the stone east. Both earlier certificates and the east contact are needed to open Earth's workshop.
7. Earth records the eastern certificate; the east stone and this certificate authorize Water's far workshop.
8. Water records the last certificate. Fire reverses the belt a second time; the west contact is now required for all three exits.
9. The party exits after the stone arrives west.

## Why each player is needed
Earth alone can break the floor, and its east workshop is in a separate lower chamber. Fire and Water must each approve the release, record the lower west certificates, and coordinate the last east and west positions.

## The insight
A cargo key cannot be in the upper and lower circuits at once. Spend the upper state only once all its obligations have been discharged. Then the same object is a reusable binary register.

## Likely failures and how players recover
The entry gate physically forbids premature destruction until both approvals exist. An incorrectly reversed belt can always be reversed back: the recovery simulation selects the east direction before the release, verifies arrival east, then reverses and finishes. The stone cannot leave the cargo bay; both end contacts are stable. The stepped one-way ledge beside the dividing wall provides a return to the right-hand drop chamber if Earth enters the cargo bay; the belt is slow, and no timed input is required.

## Shortcuts that were closed
Leaving the slab intact and refusing the final reversal both fail in scripted attempts. The three idle-at-exit attempts also fail.

## Kinship with the campaign
Fault Line uses a broken floor as a player's passage. Undermine at two players releases a boulder to a single fixed switch. Here the irreversible release replaces the upper contact with a reversible two-state conveyor register and opens complementary gates, forcing the initial approvals and lower certificates to be obtained in order.
