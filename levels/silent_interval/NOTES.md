# Silent Interval

Batch-90: 69/90

**Introduces:** the gate is held shut by a union of four long cargo contacts. The useful signal is a moving hole in that union: both stones must simultaneously be between their two contacts. It is a negative rather than positive coincidence.

## The intended solution
1. Fire launches the slow stone.
2. Earth approaches the closed silence gate, while Water waits instead of launching immediately.
3. When the small upper cue is contacted, Water launches the faster stone.
4. The two gaps in contact align; all four vetoes disappear together and Earth crosses the temporarily open gate.
5. Earth records the east certificate, authorizing both workshop gates once their corresponding stone reaches the far contact.
6. Fire and Water record both workshop certificates.
7. Both return toward their west exits; Earth waits at the last gate. All three exits remain blocked while a stone is away.
8. The two clocks expire and return both stones home. Both certificates and both home contacts authorize the exits.

## Why each player is needed
The two launch stations are separated; one launch alone leaves another veto active throughout. Earth must cross the silence gate to authorize both workshops. Both workshop certificates are needed to finish.

## The insight
Instead of making contacts coincide, align the two *absences*. A simultaneous launch fails because the stones spend different times travelling to their uncontacted intervals.

## Likely failures and how players recover
If the launches are simultaneous or a player misses the gate, return both clocks home and repeat; the two launch stations remain accessible. A recovery run deliberately launched together, waited for both to return and verified Earth's certificate was not yet set, then launched correctly and completed. A 90-tick delayed second launch also completed. Both workshop doors latch, so their later far contacts require no timed input.

## Shortcuts that were closed
Simultaneous launch and leaving one stone at home were tested and fail. All barriers reach their ceilings. All final gates require both home contacts and both workshop certificates.

## Kinship with the campaign
Ferryman uses a quorum of stationary switches. Phase Canon at this count uses electrical timer expiry order; here the useful interval is a gap between the physical positions of two cargo objects and the receiver must move during that gap. Phase Rendezvous at two players aligns positive contacts and latches them; Silent Interval aligns the absence of every veto and provides a nonlatched crossing for a third participant.
