# Dual Embargo

Batch-90: 79/90

**Introduces:** a floating stone is both the route and an irreversible electrical veto. Two independent operators must revoke both live approach permits, not merely one, and later restore them.

## The intended solution
1. Water authorizes the Fire station, while Air sets its own approach permit.
2. Fire sets the other permit; only the quorum opens Earth's stone approach.
3. Earth pushes the stone into the channel. The floor now exists, but its heavy contact vetoes the lower road.
4. Fire enters its far workshop and Earth crosses the bridge to record the first east certificate. Both certificates authorize Water's central gate.
5. Fire and Air withdraw both original permits. Because the stone remains active, revoking just one cannot reopen Water's inverted gate.
6. Water records its western certificate. Air's own inverse gate and the new certificate release Air's eastern inspection; Air records its independent west-release certificate.
7. Both approach permits are restored, opening the way back for Earth. Earth crosses back to record its west certificate.
8. Earth crosses east a third time. Both operators withdraw their permits again so Water and Air can enter their final workshops.
9. Fire, Water, and Air record their three final certificates; together with Earth's west certificate, these authorize Earth's final east exit.
10. Earth's last release opens Water's western exit.

## Why each player is needed
Earth moves the stone, Water issues the initial authorization and obtains both lower workshop certificates, Fire controls one half of the approach quorum and its own far certificate, and Air controls the other half and certifies the inspection that allows Earth to reach the western chamber. Galleries are physically separated.

## The insight
The permanent heavy signal comes at the cost of the original route. An inverted two-of-three gate does not open if only one of the two original permissions is removed. Both operators have to revoke, restore and revoke again; Air cannot inspect until its own permission has been removed.

## Likely failures and how players recover
An early withdrawal closes Earth's approach; the controls remain accessible and can be restored. A recovery replay explicitly withdrew and restored Fire's permit before proceeding and completed in 52.95 s. Keeping either permit on blocks the inverted gate, but both stations are reachable. The floating stone stays in the channel between its raised banks. Certificate gates latch. No timed inputs or narrow landing are required. Arbitrary intermediate states are not exhaustively tested.

## Shortcuts that were closed
Retaining either initial permit, or trying to pass the unplaced stone, fails in the simulator. The low roof stops jumping over the stone. Full-height doors and rock galleries prevent substituting operators.

## Kinship with the campaign
Ferry uses a stone to support a runner but not as a persistent electrical veto. Causeway at four players plugs a channel but does not force revocation of permissions. Stone Causeway at three players is the physical ancestor, with one controlling permit; here both controls must be removed to open the inverted quorum and Air's independent inspection is needed before Earth returns. Causeway-as-veto is new at this player count.
