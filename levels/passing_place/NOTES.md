# Passing Place (`passing_place`) — Water + Earth

**Introduces:** a crawlway one tile high with a stone in it. The stone is an absolute wall that only
Earth can move and only ever forwards, the one stretch of raised roof is the only place anybody can
get past it — and that same raised place is the plate that holds the far hatch open, so standing
aside and opening the way are the same act, and whoever does it cannot be the one who goes.

## The intended solution

1. **Earth** climbs the two steps into the crawlway and shunts the stone east along it. The
   crawlway is one tile high: nobody can jump in it, nobody can pass anybody, and nobody at all can
   get past the stone. Earth can only ever push, so every tile the stone moves is a tile it will
   never come back over.
2. Earth parks the stone under the far side of the niche — the one stretch where the roof lifts to
   four tiles — and walks back west to the open tile at `x=15`.
3. **Earth** jumps up onto the shelf in the niche. The plate there opens the hatch at the east end
   of the crawlway. It also *shuts* the gallery gate overhead, which is the only way home.
4. **Water** crawls east, under the shelf and under Earth — the one place a partner can be got past
   — then takes a run at the stone from `x=19` and hops it: the only place the stone can be got past
   either. Through the hatch, off the lip and down into the right hall.
5. **Water** clears the stone's empty berth, puts out the flames at `x=33` (the one thing in the
   level only Water can do) and comes back to the foot of the ladder.
6. Up the rungs to the gallery, west along it, picking up the gem — and then Water is stopped at the
   gallery gate, because Earth is still holding the shelf. **Earth has to give the shelf up**, which
   shuts the hatch it opened in step 3, and drops off to the east: the side the stone is on.
7. **Water** carries on west, falls down the shaft into the left hall, crawls the whole length of
   the crawlway again and climbs the shelf in Earth's place. The hatch opens again.
8. **Earth**, now behind the stone with somebody else holding the hatch, shunts it the last stretch,
   through the hatch and off the lip into the right hall, follows it down, and pushes it east into
   its berth at `x=28`. The stone drops into the notch onto the heavy plate and can never be pushed
   out again — which is exactly what is wanted, because that plate holds the vault at `x=35` open
   for good.
9. **Earth** jumps the berth, walks through the dead flames and the vault, and throws the lever. The
   lever is the hatch's second input: the hatch is now open whether or not anybody is on the shelf.
   That is the only thing in the level that can free whoever is holding it.
10. **Water** comes off the shelf, crawls east one last time and walks to its door in the hall.
    **Earth** goes back west to the ladder, climbs to the gallery — the gate is open again now the
    shelf is empty — and walks the length of it to its own door at the west end.

## Why each player is needed

- **Earth** is the only one who can move the stone at all, and the stone has to end up on a heavy
  plate that no player can hold while also standing in a door. Earth is also the only one who can be
  the pusher, and a pusher cannot simultaneously be the body on the shelf.
- **Water** is the only one who can put out the flames, and everything that matters at the east end
  — the vault, the lever, Water's own door — is behind them. Water is also the only other body in
  the level, so Water is the only thing that can hold the shelf while Earth pushes. Those two jobs
  are at opposite ends, which is why Water has to travel the whole loop twice.

## The insight

The shelf is the only place in the crawlway to get out of the walking line, and it is also the
switch. So the player who makes it possible for the other one to go is, by that very act, the player
who cannot go. The stone makes that permanent: it can only go forwards, so the two of them have to
work out, before any of it, which of them should be in front of it when it stops.

## Likely failures and how players recover

- **Water takes the shelf first, and Earth goes east.** Earth can do nothing out there: it cannot
  put the flames out and the vault wants a weight it has not brought. It costs the loop — Earth
  climbs the ladder, crosses the gallery (Water has to come off the shelf to let it through the
  gate) and drops back into the left hall.
- **Earth pushes the stone the whole way at once.** The hatch is shut, so the stone jams against it
  at `x=23`, under a roof one tile high where nobody can get past it any more. Nothing is lost:
  whoever is west simply takes the shelf, and Earth pushes it the rest of the way. Earth only has to
  be careful that Water is not the one left on the eastern side of it.
- **Earth hops over the stone in the niche and goes east empty-handed.** The stone is now behind
  Earth and Earth can never pull it. Recover round the loop: ladder, gallery, shaft, and Earth is
  behind the stone again. Costs about twenty seconds.
- **Earth stays on the shelf when Water reaches the gallery gate.** Water waits at a shut gate until
  Earth works out that it has to let go. Nothing breaks; it is the step the level is about.
- **Somebody waits in the stone's berth.** Earth standing in the notch does open the vault — but the
  door is seven tiles away and shuts in under half a second, so nobody can use it. Step out and
  deliver the stone properly.

## Shortcuts that were closed

- **Jumping from a partner's head.** The only height that matters is the lip back up into the
  crawlway, four tiles above the right hall, which a boost can reach. It needs both players to be in
  the right hall at once, and that state cannot happen until the lever has already been thrown — by
  which time the hatch is open anyway. The gallery is twelve tiles up and the west shaft eleven:
  both are out of reach of any stack.
- **Getting past the stone anywhere but the niche.** The crawlway is one tile high everywhere else,
  which forbids jumping outright, and a boulder in a one-tile corridor cannot be hopped even with
  jump held.
- **Getting past each other.** Same roof. The shelf is the only place a body can be out of the
  walking line, and it is three tiles wide with four tiles of headroom, so the climb needs no
  precision.
- **Opening the vault without the stone.** The heavy plate takes mass 2 or more, so Earth's own body
  does open it — but the plate is seven tiles from the door, far more than the twenty-four ticks the
  door takes to shut, and the other player cannot be in the hall at that point: getting there needs
  the hatch open, which needs a body on the shelf, and there are only two bodies.
- **Holding a gate open with a body.** The hatch is one tile in a one-tile corridor: whoever stands
  under it fills the gap, so nobody slips through behind them. The vault and the flames are three
  tiles under rock up to the ceiling, and the gallery gate fills its corridor from floor to roof.
- **Going east any other way.** The gallery runs over the top of the whole level but its only
  descent is the ladder in the right hall, and the left hall cannot reach it. The loop runs one way:
  east through the hatch, west over the gallery.
- **Unwinnable states.** There are none that were found. No liquid is used anywhere, so nobody can
  die. The stone only ever moves east, but the loop means Earth can always get behind it again, and
  its berth is a notch it cannot be pushed out of. The gallery gate is shut only while a partner is
  on the shelf, and that partner can always step off.

## Kinship with the campaign

Portcullis is the nearest: a plate too far from its gate, so one player has to hold it while the
other goes, and the holder cannot be the one who gets through. Battering Ram is the nearest for the
stone — a boulder nobody present can push. What is different here is that the holding place and the
passing place are the same tile: the crawlway is one tile high, so the shelf is the only spot where
a body can be out of the way, and that spot is the switch. Portcullis's holder stays behind for a
few seconds; this one stays behind for a lap of the level, and has to be let out by a lever that can
only be reached from the far side.
