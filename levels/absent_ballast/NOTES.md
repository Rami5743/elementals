# Absent Ballast

Batch-90: 80/90

**Introduces:** the rope remembers the weight of a jumper until landing, allowing the rider to leave a loaded contact behind in midair. The required draught is temporarily revoked for a separate inspection while the rider waits on the pan.

## The intended solution
1. Air grants the initial draught permission, Earth holds the first heavy bellows, and Water rides the draught to the gallery.
2. Water boards the pan, outweighing the fixed counterweight and lowering the boulder onto the contact.
3. Fire enters the inspection workshop only after that low contact arrives and records the low-pan certificate.
4. Air must withdraw its initial draught permit, thereby opening its own inverted passage, then records the independent hatch certificate.
5. Air restores the draught permit. The low pan and both certificates open Earth's hatch; Earth crosses from the first bellows to the second. Water stays on the pan.
6. With the draught restored, Water jumps off the pan and into the column. The rope continues counting the airborne weight, so the boulder holds the upper gate open.
7. Water lands beyond the gate; the rope unloads and rises, opening the inverted inner gate. Water records the arrival certificate.
8. Air withdraws the draught permit again to obtain the far western certificate, unlocking Water's final inspection.
9. Water records the upper certificate; Fire crosses to the western workshop and records the final authorization.
10. Air returns east; Earth and Water reach their final doors.

## Why each player is needed
Earth is the only body that can operate the heavy bellows. Water alone reaches the loaded rope and completes the airborne passage. Fire and Air are isolated in separate workshops; Fire certifies the low pan, and Air provides and revokes the draught permission to certify the hatch and the upper arrival. All four are required by the exit checks.

## The insight
Leaving a pan is not the same as unloading it. The party must deliberately switch off the draught while Water is still safely standing on the pan, and then restore it before Water flies. The return phase requires revoking that permission once more.

## Likely failures and how players recover
Flying without first boarding cannot open the upper gate. Landing prematurely in the hall unloads the rope, but the second bellows and draught allow Water to wait for the pan to reset, return to it and repeat the climb. The recovery replay deliberately did so and finished in 61.35 s. Air can always restore the permission from its station; a separate early withdrawal replay completed in 40.95 s. A failed leap falls onto a safe floor; the return shaft beyond the gallery also leads to the hall. No timed input or narrow landing is required. The three-tile-open span includes the draught itself; the actual gap to the draught is two tiles, and the broad column catches the jumper. Arbitrary intermediate states are not exhaustively tested.

## Shortcuts that were closed
The direct flight, landing in the hall before attempting the upper gate without reboarding, and retaining the draught permit instead of inspecting fail in simulation. Full-height doors, separate galleries and the heavy bellows prevent substitution.

## Kinship with the campaign
Slow Fall uses airborne contact as a timer; here airborne weight is mechanically remembered, not a countdown. Adapted from the two-player Ballast, with two additional isolated workshops whose inspections and complementary permits are necessary for the handoff. Rope-keeps-the-jumper is new at four players.
