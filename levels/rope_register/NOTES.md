# Rope Register

**Introduces:** A balanced pulley is a mechanical memory: a rider writes its low position, removing the snatch block erases it, and restoring the snatch block without a rider retains the high position.

## The intended solution

1. Water turns A on to open the upper passage for Fire.
2. Fire boards beside the boulder. The ratio is 2; Fire and the boulder outweigh the opposite fixed weight of 6, so the loaded platform descends.
3. Fire leaves it at the bottom, sets the lower lever, and boards again. Without Fire the rope balances and retains the low state. The low contact gives Water access to B.
4. Water turns B on. A and B together disengage the snatch block. The opposing weight raises Fire and the stone. The high contact and lower lever latch Water's second gate.
5. Fire reaches the upper right lever and sets it. Water returns to B, disengages B and returns to A to turn it off. That opens the inverted passage.
6. Fire boards again and writes the low position. Water crosses the latched final gate and sets C. Fire returns to the lower platform.
7. Water restores B and A, erasing the low position. Fire rises with the stone and passes the upper gate to the left side.
8. Water turns A off again. This time the stone alone balances the pulley at the **high** position. The inverted passage is open, and the high contact and C permit the last exit. Fire crosses the recovery lift at the upper stop and reaches the exit opened by C.

## Why each player is needed

Fire adds exactly the extra weight and operates the two levers on different storeys. Water controls the ratio and supplies the permissions from its own gallery. The low and high contacts are heavy; Fire alone cannot substitute for the stone. Neither can enter the other's gallery.

## The insight

The same switch configuration can give two different outputs, depending on the previous position of the pulley. The stone is a resource for two mutually exclusive claims. The crucial last move is to restore the balancing ratio only after Fire has stepped off the high platform, instead of leaving the apparent power switch on.

## Likely failures and how players recover

- Erasing while Fire is below and not on the pulley would strand Fire. The independent two-tile-wide recall lift at the left end of the gallery permits a return to the upper floor. It has a five-second dwell.
- Turning A off too early closes Fire's upper passage. Water can return to A because the first gate is latched; A can be restored.
- Keeping B off prevents the required high state from being written. Return to B and A, restore both, then release the rider before switching back to balance.
- The player may fall into either shaft; the floor is solid and the lift can be approached again. This is intended as a recoverable route, not a precision jump. The recovery path has not been exhaustively tested for every physical state.

## Shortcuts that were closed

The two scripts try the direct path and leaving the rope at the bottom instead of erasing. Both fail. The contacts need heavy mass; Fire and Water cannot push the boulder. The galleries are isolated, doors reach the ceiling, and the stone is not a substitute for the missing remote lever.

## Kinship with the campaign

Weighbridge changes mechanical advantage to select a destination. Here the equal-weight equilibrium stores a previous destination, and the same lever configuration has different effects before and after the memory is written. It is not just a transport lift.
