# Draught (`draught`) — Fire + Earth + Air

**Introduces:** a heavy plate that has to stay down for good, so the boulder — not a player —
becomes the machine's switch.

1. **Solution.** Earth hops for its gem and shoulders the boulder along the hall floor onto the
   heavy plate; the draught in the shaft starts and never stops. Fire climbs onto Air's head and
   Air steps into the draught, carrying Fire sixteen tiles up past the neutral gem into the loft.
   Fire steps off, stands on the torch and lights it: the gate across the loft and the hatch down
   in the hall open together. Fire takes its gem, Air takes its own, and all three walk to their
   doors — Fire and Air through the loft gate, Earth through the hatch below.

2. **Roles.** Earth is the only mass that matters to a heavy plate and the only thing that can
   move the boulder. Air is the only one the draught lifts. Fire is the only one who can light the
   torch, and the only reason to carry anyone up at all.

3. **Why each is necessary.** The plate is heavy, so neither Fire nor Air can start the draught,
   and the draught at its default strength lifts nobody but Air. Both doors have the torch as
   their only input, and the loft gate is four tiles with the roof above it. `check` confirms all
   three counterfactuals.

4. **The insight.** Earth never goes up at all: its door is on the ground. The level is built so
   that the player who starts the machine is not the player the machine is for — and the boulder
   is what lets Earth walk away from the plate.

5. **Likely failures and recovery.** The boulder cannot be lost: the plate is against the left end
   of the hall and the boulder stops there. Fire slipping off Air's head drops it back into the
   hall, where it can walk over and climb on again; the draught keeps running, so there is no
   second setup to redo. There is no liquid and nothing deadly in the level.

- **Shortcuts considered and blocked.** The loft is sixteen tiles up with solid rock either side
  of the shaft, out of reach of any jump, head-stand or three-player tower. The hatch and the loft
  gate both run to the roof. The torch sits six tiles clear of the gate so that nobody can brush
  it in passing, and Earth, who cannot be carried by the draught at this strength, has no business
  up there anyway.
